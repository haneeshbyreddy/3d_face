01

How big should the thing be?

130 mm, about the height of a coffee mug.

Not because it prints best, but because the printer is not what is scarce. Our hands are. A 50 mm piece and a 130 mm one need the same scan repair, sanding and painting, so the bigger one earns far more for the same work.

Why not print small? Fifteen keychains fit on a plate, only two busts do.

Plate space is free; the machine runs unattended overnight on about a rupee of electricity. Hands are not free. Fifteen small pieces per plate is fifteen times the sanding, not fifteen times the business — a keychain returns ₹539 an hour, a 130 mm bust ₹894.

One consequence to flag now: a 130 mm bust is about 79 mm wide, which will not fit the 77 mm-deep plate of a small Mars-class printer. Choosing this size forces the larger machine in answer 04.

Full numbers. 50 mm: 11 ml resin, 15 per plate, 1.0 h work, ₹61 material, sell ₹600. 90 mm: 35 ml, 4 per plate, 1.7 h, ₹145, sell ₹1,500. 130 mm: 73 ml, 2 per plate, 2.5 h, ₹265, sell ₹2,500. 180 mm: 140 ml, 1 per plate, 3.5 h, ₹475, sell ₹4,000.

02

Which kind of 3D printer?

Resin. It prints three faces in the time filament prints one.

A printer builds an object one flat slice at a time. Filament draws each slice line by line, so two faces take twice as long. Resin flashes the whole slice at once: the plate is either full or it isn’t.

Assumptions. 130 mm hollow bust, 2 mm walls. Resin at 0.05 mm layers, about 2.5 s per layer on a modern tilt-release machine, two per Saturn-class plate. Filament at 0.16 mm layers on a fast machine, about six hours each. A 130 mm head sliced at 0.05 mm is 2,600 slices.

03

Is an iPhone scan accurate enough?

Yes, and it is the weak link, which is why we buy plate size, not resolution.

The phone lands about 0.86 mm off a clinical scanner — 0.49 mm on the statue once shrunk, and primer fills another 0.1 mm. Every resin printer on the market already resolves ten to thirty times finer.

Where resin still beats filament, and what the scan always breaks

Resin earns its price on surface, not accuracy: filament leaves visible ridges across a cheek, resin does not. On raw accuracy both are already finer than the phone.

Scan quality is not uniform across a face. Skin and bone come out well; hair, spectacle frames and thin jewellery break every time and are rebuilt by hand in Blender, about 25 minutes a face. That skill lives in the software, not the machine.

The 0.86 mm figure is from a clinical validation of iPhone TrueDepth against a 3dMD stereophotogrammetry system, with 97% of facial landmarks inside 2 mm.

05

What does one face cost, and when does it pay back?

₹265 of material sells for ₹2,500. Thirty sales clear the setup.

Material is almost noise. The real cost is 2.5 hours of hand-work per piece — also the ceiling on how many we can sell in a week. The printer runs itself overnight; the finishing does not.

06

What could go wrong?

The risk is demand, not the technology.

A firm in Rangareddy already sells 3D selfies at ₹3,599, with others in Chennai and Delhi. The idea is not new and nobody has scaled it. Worth understanding before assuming we would.

Kills the business

Nobody buys

Competitors exist and stay small. Either the market is thin, or the product is a novelty people admire and do not buy.

Kills the business

We are not selling what they sell

The market sells full-colour gypsum from ₹25 lakh machines; we would sell painted grey resin. A better object if sold as a bronze or marble sculpture, but never as the same thing.

Costs us time

Hair and glasses break every scan

Infrared depth sensing fails on dark hair, spectacle frames and thin jewellery. Every scan needs hand sculpting before it can print. Someone has to learn that.

Health

Resin is a sensitiser

Skin allergies that get worse with exposure, and fumes that need real ventilation. This lives in a utility room with an exhaust fan, not on a dining table.

Budgeted, but surprises people

The screen is a consumable

LCD panels last 2,000–4,000 hours and cost ₹3,000–8,000. Plan on one a year at volume.
